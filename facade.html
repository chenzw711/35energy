The façade is the array.

A curtain-wall panel that generates. 880 × 480 mm, 88 W, 208 W/m² — and a colour, texture and transmittance the architect can actually specify.

What it is

Not a module bolted to a wall.

Conventional BIPV is a compromise: a framed module hung in front of a building, with the architect asked to design around it. This is the other direction — the generating unit becomes the cladding element, so the elevation is designed first and the electrical layer is built into it.

Two glass panes, 2.0 mm each, laminated around 210 × 105 mm mono cells. Front and rear glass means the panel behaves as a building product — impact, fire and wind-load performance are decided at the glass, not added afterwards. The busbar-free cell process carries over from our main line, which is why the efficiency holds at 20.83% in a format this small.

The colour is not a film. Ink-blue and gilded gold are produced in the glass build-up, so the finish does not delaminate, fade at the edges, or change the panel's fire classification.

Customisation

Three variables the architect controls.

Every one of them costs output. That trade is the whole design conversation on a BIPV project, and it is better had before the façade drawings are frozen.

Engineering reality

What a vertical façade actually produces.

A south-facing vertical plane receives far less annual irradiation than a tilted array at the same site — and much more in winter than in summer. BIPV projects fail on yield expectations more often than on hardware.

We will run the orientation study before quoting. Send the building elevation drawings and the site coordinates. If the façade is north-facing or heavily shaded, we will say so rather than sell panels into a bad elevation.
  • 01

    Building code comes first

    The panel must satisfy fire, impact and wind-load requirements before it generates anything. Glass build-up and fixing detail are set by the façade engineer, not by us.

  • 02

    Cavity temperature cuts output

    A ventilated rainscreen behind the panel keeps the cell cooler. A sealed cavity traps heat and costs yield all summer — a detail worth arguing about at design stage.

  • 03

    Cabling belongs in the detail

    Every panel needs a connection route that is not visible on the elevation. Junction space has to be designed into the mullion, not retrofitted on site.

  • 04

    String design is not standard

    Facade arrays are small, irregular and split across orientations. Expect more MPPT channels and shorter strings than a roof array of the same capacity.
