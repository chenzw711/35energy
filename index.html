Why heterojunction

The temperature coefficient is where the yield actually lives.

A module datasheet is measured at 25 °C. A working array rarely is. In high-irradiance markets the cell junction runs 30–45 °C above ambient, and every hundredth of a percent per degree compounds across the whole array, every day, for 30 years.

  • 01

    −0.24%/°C on Pmax

    Measured on our G12-0BB cell platform. At 70 °C junction temperature this is roughly 11% output retained versus 25 °C, against a materially steeper curve for mainstream tunnel-oxide cells.

  • 02

    Bifaciality up to 95%

    Dual-glass construction with a transparent rear. On a reflective ground surface the rear side contributes real kWh — which is why we size our utility module around it.

  • 03

    1% first-year degradation

    Then 0.4%/yr linear to year 30. Lower thermal stress and a lower-temperature process are the two reasons the curve stays flat.

Manufacturing

A single-purpose HJT line, not a multi-technology compromise.

Our Meishan facility runs heterojunction only. That focus is what lets us push wafer thickness, paste consumption and cell efficiency at the same time instead of trading one for another.

  • A

    100 µm wafers in mass production

    Thinner silicon means less material per watt and a lower embodied carbon figure — both increasingly requested in European tenders.

  • B

    0BB busbar-free metallisation

    Removing the busbars cuts silver paste consumption by roughly 30% while raising the active area available for generation.

  • C

    20% silver-coated copper paste

    Already scaled on the line, with pure-copper paste and indium-free rear targets in qualification.

  • D

    33.45% tandem cell in the lab

    Perovskite-on-HJT stacked cell, verified in-house. This is the next step, not a shipping product — treat it as a technology roadmap indicator.
